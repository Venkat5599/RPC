The keeper cannot lie

execute_exit does not accept an assertion that a policy fired. It accepts evidence, and re-evaluates the predicate on-chain against live state. The keeper degrades from a trusted actor to an untrusted relayer.

The keeper cannot steal

The destination is not a parameter. It is the owner recorded in the vault, so a fully compromised guardian can neither redirect nor spend. Revocation is one transaction, with no delay and no quorum.

What this does not do yet

Stated here rather than discovered in Q&A.

  • If a protocol pauses withdrawals, RIPCORD cannot exit either. The window is between the first malicious transaction and the pause. On Drift that was hours wide — but it is a real ceiling.
  • Devnet, not mainnet. The program is deployed and demonstrated, but a mainnet program ID does not exist yet.
  • No backtested number yet. The replay engine is built and the report refuses to estimate one. It needs an archival RPC endpoint.
  • The demo exit was proposed by a human. The engine that watches a pre-execution stream and proposes automatically needs sponsor access that does not exist yet.
  • No latency claim. Simulation costs more than it saves, deliberately. The opponent is a human incident-response loop, not a colocated bot.
  • One policy. Authority change only. Oracle staleness and outflow rate are roadmap, and one of those three is honestly weaker than the others.
